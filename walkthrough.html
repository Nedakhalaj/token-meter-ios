Part three

What a widget is

You already know these. On iPhone, long-press the home screen, tap +, and add the little Weather or Calendar square. That's a widget.

CClaude42%
OCodex88%
DDrive71%

Token Meter's widget sits on your home screen and shows the same bars, so you can check your quota without opening anything.

The important rule: the widget never talks to the internet itself. The app fetches the numbers and saves them to the phone. The widget just reads that saved copy and draws it. That keeps the two from fighting over who fetches what.

On iOS you'd build this with WidgetKit. It's a genuinely separate skill from normal SwiftUI, with its own restrictions. Leave it until the main app works.

The one row that changes your plan

Android lets an app run forever in the background, checking every 15 minutes like clockwork. iOS does not allow this. The closest thing, BGAppRefreshTask, runs when iOS decides — maybe 20 minutes, maybe six hours, maybe not until tomorrow.

So the iOS version can't be a copy. It fetches when you open the app, and when the widget's timeline refreshes. Sometimes the numbers will be a bit old, and the design has to say so honestly — a small "updated 2h ago" label rather than pretending it's live.

That's a product decision, and it's yours to make before any code gets written.

Part six

Where to actually start

In order. Don't skip ahead — each step makes the next one obvious.

  1. Build the Dashboard with fake dataA hard-coded list of two accounts. No internet, no login, no saving. Just SwiftUI drawing the cards you see above. This teaches you the shape of the data.
  2. Add one real provider — OpenRouterNot Claude. OpenRouter is the easiest: you paste a key, the app makes one plain request. No web view, no codes, no token refreshing. Get real numbers on screen once.
  3. Save things between launchesKey in the Keychain, cached numbers on disk. Close the app, reopen it, see your data still there.
  4. Add Settings and the ··· menuRename, remove, refresh, pick an interval. Small screens, quick wins, and now the app feels finished.
  5. Then the hard providersCodex's device code, then Claude's web view. Both involve real authentication work. By now you'll have the vocabulary for it.
  6. Then the widgetLast. It reads what's already on disk, so it only makes sense once there's something there to read.
